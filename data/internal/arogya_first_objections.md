# Arogya First — Objection Handling

Version 1.6 | Effective 2025-04-01 | Sales operations

Approved responses only. Each response must stay factual and must not guarantee
claim outcomes. Where a caller pushes beyond these answers, escalate.

## "It is too expensive"

Acknowledge the concern, then reframe against the deductible route. A Secure
Top-up of Rs 50 lakh over a Rs 5 lakh deductible costs Rs 6,800 a year for a 35
year old, which is less than a Rs 10 lakh individual policy at Rs 9,400, because
the employer or existing cover absorbs the first Rs 5 lakh. Also mention Zone B
and Zone C pricing where the caller is outside a metro, and the 80D deduction.

Do not offer discounts. Premium is filed and cannot be negotiated on a call.

## "I already have cover from my employer"

Employer cover typically ends when employment ends, is often Rs 3 to 5 lakh, and
usually excludes parents. Recommend a Secure Top-up above the employer cover as
the cheapest way to raise the total limit, and note that a personal policy keeps
waiting periods running so that continuity is preserved if the job changes.

## "I am young and healthy, I do not need this"

Two factual points. Waiting periods run from the policy start, so buying earlier
means pre-existing and specified illness periods are already served when they are
eventually needed. Premium is set by entry age band, so entry at 30 costs Rs
9,400 against Rs 14,900 at 45 for the same Rs 10 lakh cover.

Do not use fear-based framing or cite mortality statistics.

## "Insurance companies reject claims anyway"

Acknowledge that rejections happen and give the two most common causes:
non-disclosure of an existing condition at application, and claiming inside a
waiting period. Explain that full disclosure at application is the single
strongest protection, and that cashless pre-authorisation is decided within 60
minutes so the position is known before treatment starts.

Never state a claim settlement percentage on a call.

## "I will think about it and call back"

Accept it without pressure. Offer to hold an indicative quote and book a
callback at a stated time. Confirm the callback slot verbally and create the
lead so the quote basis is preserved.

## "Why is the senior citizen premium so high?"

Explain the components factually: claim frequency and severity rise with age, a
20% co-payment already reduces the premium from what it would otherwise be, and a
pre-policy check-up is required. Where the caller is price-sensitive, the Rs 5
lakh cover amount with Senior Care is the entry point at Rs 28,300.

## "Can you guarantee my diabetes will be covered?"

No guarantee may be given. State the rule: a declared pre-existing condition
becomes claimable after the pre-existing disease waiting period of continuous
cover, and that the final terms depend on underwriting. Offer underwriter review
rather than an answer on the call.

## "Send me everything on WhatsApp and I will decide"

Confirm that a summary can be sent, take consent for contact, and still capture
the mandatory qualification fields before ending the call, because a quote cannot
be prepared without age, city, members and declared conditions.

## "I do not want to share my medical details on a recorded call"

Respect the refusal. Explain that declared conditions determine which product
applies and that the alternative is a callback from a licensed advisor. Create
the lead as incomplete and escalate rather than pressing.
